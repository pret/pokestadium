ENG: Over the past few years (since 2020), I've been following several reverse-engineering projects on 90s video games — the games of my youth. As a software developer, I've been impressed by the ability of people who've done that on games like [Super Mario 64](https://github.com/n64decomp/sm64), [Diddy Kong Racing](https://github.com/DavidSM64/Diddy-Kong-Racing), or [Devilution (Diablo)](https://github.com/galaxyhaxz/devilution). Above all, the time those people have put into those projects.

Personally, I believe a software developer should keep a constant activity of learning and curiosity, in step with the times we're living in. That's why I'm forking [pret/pokestadium](https://github.com/pret/pokestadium) (extraordinary work) to complete the phase of understanding, rewriting, and documenting the existing C code. I'll be doing it using Artificial Intelligence tools — something that today, especially in open source and community-driven projects, seems to generate a certain backlash, a feeling I don't entirely share.

If tools appear that let us get closer to the goal — cutting down what used to take a handful of people almost a decade into maybe just a few years — they're welcome. That's why I believe this project should be AI-friendly.
